Most med spas offering PRP have a marketing problem hiding in plain sight: patients who've already searched "PRP facial near me" or "hair loss treatment [city]" are choosing a competitor — not because your results are worse, but because your competitor shows up first, follows up faster, and communicates more clearly.

Platelet-rich plasma has become one of the most searched aesthetic treatments in the country. PRP facials, PRP hair restoration, and PRP combined with microneedling all draw patients who are ready to spend — and who return repeatedly for maintenance. Yet most med spas market PRP as an afterthought, lumped under a generic "treatments" page with a paragraph of explanation.

This guide gives you the full playbook: how to build your PRP service around what patients actually search for, how to get those searches to land on your practice, and how to convert that traffic into booked consultations — and then into long-term patients.

## What PRP Marketing Requires That Other Treatments Don't

PRP is a biological treatment using the patient's own blood plasma concentrated with growth factors. That distinction creates a unique marketing challenge: you're selling a process — not a device brand name like Morpheus8 or a household name like Botox.

This has two implications. First, patients need more education before they're ready to book. They're not just deciding "where to get PRP." They're deciding "whether to get PRP at all." Your marketing must move them from awareness to conviction, not just from competitor to your practice.

Second, HIPAA applies to everything. Patient photos, results shared in testimonials, and any before/after imagery must meet strict privacy standards. [HIPAA-compliant social media marketing](/blog/hipaa-compliant-social-media-marketing) covers the compliance rules in detail — make sure your whole team understands them before you post anything.

## Build Your PRP Menu Before You Market

You can't effectively market a service you haven't clearly packaged. Before running a single ad, define your PRP offerings and price them clearly.

**PRP Facial (Vampire Facial):** The classic entry point. Topical application of PRP after microneedling. Prices typically range $600–$1,200 per session, with best results from a series of 3.

**PRP Hair Restoration:** Scalp injections to stimulate follicle activity. Typical protocol: 3–4 sessions spaced 4–6 weeks apart, then maintenance every 6–12 months. Pricing: $800–$1,500 per session.

**PRP + RF Microneedling:** Premium combination. PRP applied after [radiofrequency microneedling](/blog/rf-microneedling-morpheus8-marketing-med-spa-guide) for enhanced tissue regeneration and collagen stimulation. Price point: $1,200–$2,000 per session.

**PRP Biostimulator Combinations:** Pairing PRP with Sculptra or Radiesse for synergistic collagen induction. This is a premium, result-driven offering for patients 45+ seeking deeper rejuvenation.

For each offering, define: the ideal candidate profile, the treatment protocol, expected results timeline, and maintenance schedule. Your marketing content — every page, ad, and social post — flows directly from this clarity.

## Who Books PRP (and How to Find Them)

Understanding your patient is step one of every marketing decision.

**PRP Facial Patient:**

- Age 32–55, predominantly female
- Interested in natural, "clean" beauty approaches
- Concerns: fine lines, skin texture, hyperpigmentation, dull complexion
- Search behavior: "natural skin rejuvenation," "PRP facial near me," "vampire facial results"
- Typical HHI: $80,000+
- Often exploring alternatives to injectables or seeking to enhance injectable results

**PRP Hair Restoration Patient:**

- Age 30–60, mixed gender (growing male market, significant female segment post-partum or hormonal)
- Concerns: thinning, hairline recession, post-partum shedding
- Search behavior: "hair loss treatment," "PRP for hair loss [city]," "stop hair thinning naturally"
- High purchase intent — hair loss creates emotional urgency
- Often researches extensively before booking

**Key insight:** PRP hair restoration patients convert at a higher rate once they book a consultation because the problem (hair loss) is visually apparent and emotionally significant. They need education on your website, but they close faster in consultation than facial patients.

## Google Ads for PRP: The Campaigns That Perform

PRP patients search with intent. They've already decided they want a biological, non-invasive approach — they're selecting a provider. Paid search captures this ready-to-book traffic.

**Campaign 1: PRP Facial / Vampire Facial**

Top-performing keywords:

- "PRP facial [city]"
- "vampire facial near me"
- "PRP skin treatment [city]"
- "platelet rich plasma facial"

Negative keywords to add immediately: "DIY," "home," "dermaroller" (captures budget-seekers, not your patient), "PRP joint" (medical orthopedic patients)

**Campaign 2: PRP Hair Restoration**

Top-performing keywords:

- "PRP hair loss treatment [city]"
- "PRP for thinning hair"
- "hair restoration med spa"
- "platelet rich plasma hair"

This campaign typically has lower competition than hair transplant surgery terms — you're targeting the non-surgical patient.

**Landing page rules:** Each campaign needs its own landing page, not your homepage. PRP facial traffic → PRP facial page with patient-focused benefits, FAQ, and a single CTA ("Book Your Consultation"). PRP hair traffic → Hair restoration page with before/after education, protocol explanation, and consultation form.

For a complete paid search framework, see [Google Ads for Medical Spas](/blog/google-ads-medical-spas-guide).

**Performance expectations:**

- Average cost per lead for PRP: $35–$90 (lower than injectables due to less competition)
- Average consultation booking rate: 30–45% of qualified leads
- Average consult-to-treatment conversion: 55–70% (high because patients are motivated)

## Local SEO for PRP: Organic Traffic That Compounds

Paid search gets you immediate traffic. Local SEO builds a patient acquisition asset that grows over time.

**Your Google Business Profile (GBP):** PRP should be listed explicitly as a service in your GBP, not buried in your website. Use the "Services" section to list "PRP Facial," "PRP Hair Restoration," and "PRP Microneedling" as separate line items with descriptions. Patients searching in Google Maps for PRP need to find you.

**Dedicated PRP pages on your website:** Create separate pages for PRP facial and PRP hair restoration. Each page should include:

- What PRP is and how it works (education converts skeptics)
- Who is a good candidate
- What to expect (before, during, recovery)
- Expected results timeline (with realistic language — not "guaranteed")
- FAQs
- Consultation CTA

**Blog content for PRP:** Informational blog posts drive organic traffic from patients researching before they're ready to book:

- "PRP Facial vs. Traditional Microneedling: Which Is Right for You?"
- "How Many PRP Hair Sessions Do You Need? A Realistic Timeline"
- "PRP Facial Recovery: What to Expect the First Week"

These articles rank for long-tail searches and build topical authority on PRP at your practice.

**Local schema markup:** Tag your PRP services with MedicalProcedure and MedicalClinic schema. This helps Google understand and feature your content in AI Overviews and local packs.

## HIPAA-Safe Social Media Strategy for PRP

Social media is where PRP visual results can drive enormous interest — but every post requires HIPAA compliance. No patient photos without explicit written authorization. No before/after images sourced from the internet. No patient-identifiable information in any post.

**What you can post:**

- Provider-facing PRP process imagery (preparation, centrifuge, application on yourself or a consenting team member)
- Educational video content: "What is PRP and how does it work?"
- Lifestyle/result imagery from consenting patients with signed authorization
- Staff education: "Our providers are trained in the latest PRP protocols for skin and hair"
- FAQ video series: short-form answers to common PRP questions

**Instagram/TikTok content pillars for PRP:**

1. **Education:** "Here's what happens during a PRP facial" (process video)
2. **Myth-busting:** "No, PRP is not painful — here's what it actually feels like"
3. **Results storytelling:** Patient testimonial (authorized) or provider commentary on why they love PRP outcomes
4. **Social proof:** "Questions we get asked every day about PRP hair restoration"
5. **Behind the scenes:** Lab/preparation imagery that conveys clinical credibility

For hair restoration, before/after content is especially powerful — with consent. Male pattern baldness is visible and measurable; authorized patient results photos are among the highest-converting content in this niche.

## Converting PRP Consultations: What Closes the Room

PRP consultations have a distinct dynamic. Patients arrive educated but uncertain — they've done research but want you to confirm they're a good candidate and set realistic expectations.

**The consultation structure that converts:**

1. **Validate their research** ("You've done a great job researching this — PRP has solid clinical evidence behind it")
2. **Assess their candidacy** (skin type, hair loss pattern, lifestyle factors — be honest if they're not ideal candidates; this builds trust)
3. **Walk through the process** (what they'll experience, not just what the treatment is)
4. **Set the results timeline realistically** — PRP results develop over 60–90 days. Patients who understand this don't become unhappy patients at week 3.
5. **Present options, not ultimatums** — Offer single session, series, and combination packages. Let them choose their investment level.

**Automated follow-up after consultation:** If they don't book in the room, your follow-up sequence makes the difference. The sequence should run:

- Day 1: "It was great meeting you — here's the information we discussed" (summary email with FAQ)
- Day 3: Educational content ("PRP hair restoration: 6-month results from patients like you")
- Day 7: Soft offer ("We have a few consultations available this week if you'd like to move forward")
- Day 14: Final touchpoint with a specific CTA

This is covered in depth in [automated follow-up sequences](/blog/automated-follow-up-sequences-guide).

## Before/After Gallery: Your Most Powerful PRP Marketing Asset

A well-managed before/after gallery is the single highest-converting content asset for PRP. Patients searching for PRP will look at your results before they look at your pricing.

The rules for a compliant, effective gallery:

- Written, signed HIPAA authorization for every image used
- Consistent photography: same lighting, same angle, same distance — for clinical credibility
- Authentic results: no filters, no Photoshop beyond standard color correction
- Result labels: include treatment (PRP facial series of 3, PRP hair restoration 4 sessions) and timeframe (results at 90 days)
- Diverse representation: show different skin tones, ages, genders, and starting conditions

For the full framework on building a results gallery that converts without violating compliance, see [before/after gallery best practices](/blog/before-after-gallery-best-practices).

**Gallery placement matters:** Your before/after gallery should live on dedicated PRP service pages, not just in a generic "Results" section. Patients looking at PRP facial results don't want to scroll through hair restoration photos to find what's relevant to them.

## Package Design for Higher PRP Revenue

PRP is rarely a one-and-done treatment. Packages increase average transaction value and commit the patient to the full protocol that actually delivers results.

**PRP Facial Packages:**

- Starter: 1 PRP Facial (for first-timers unsure of the experience) — $750
- Recommended Protocol: 3 PRP Facials spaced 4 weeks apart — $1,800 (save $450)
- Elite Rejuvenation: 3 PRP Facials + 1 RF Microneedling — $2,800 (save $700)

**PRP Hair Packages:**

- Initial Protocol: 3 PRP Hair Sessions — $2,500 (save $400)
- Full Restoration: 4 PRP Hair Sessions + 1-year follow-up session — $3,800 (best value framing)

**Cross-sell opportunities:**

- Botox/neurotoxins + PRP facial (complement, not compete — explain the synergy)
- Growth factor skincare take-home products after PRP facial
- Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) caps between PRP hair sessions

For more on packaging strategy, see [treatment package marketing for aesthetic practices](/blog/treatment-package-marketing-aesthetic-practices) and [upselling and cross-selling strategies](/blog/upselling-cross-selling-small-business-guide).

## Reactivating Lapsed PRP Patients

Most PRP patients need maintenance sessions — they just forget or delay. A structured reactivation campaign recovers this revenue without new acquisition costs.

**Reactivation triggers:**

- 6 months after last PRP facial → "Time for your touch-up"
- 12 months after PRP hair session → "Your annual maintenance is due"
- 90 days with no follow-up booking after completing a series

**Reactivation message framework:** Reference their specific results, remind them of the maintenance protocol they committed to, and offer a time-limited booking incentive. This is the same framework used in [patient reactivation campaigns](/blog/patient-reactivation-campaigns-aesthetic-practices) — the difference is that PRP reactivation focuses on treatment efficacy, not promotions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is PRP different from other injectable treatments like Botox or filler?

PRP uses the patient's own blood plasma to stimulate the body's natural healing and collagen production — it's not a neurotoxin or a filler. The effects develop gradually over 60–90 days rather than immediately. Patients are often interested in PRP as a more "natural" option or as a complement to other treatments.

### Can I market PRP on social media without violating HIPAA?

Yes, with proper protocols. You need written patient authorization for any patient photos or testimonials. Educational and process content that doesn't identify any patient is generally safe. Review your state's specific requirements and consult your compliance advisor — HIPAA applies, but so do state-level medical privacy regulations.

### How long before PRP marketing shows a positive ROI?

Paid search campaigns can show results within the first 30 days if landing pages convert well. Organic SEO takes 3–6 months to build momentum. Existing patient reactivation campaigns typically show ROI within 2–3 weeks of launch.

### Should I target men or women more heavily for PRP hair restoration?

Target both. Female hair loss (hormonal, post-partum, diffuse thinning) is underserved and patients are often more distressed than male patients. Male patients have higher purchase intent once they engage. Run separate creatives — the pain points and language are very different.

### What's the most common reason PRP consultations don't convert?

Unclear results expectations. Patients who believe PRP will work "like Botox" (visible results in days) become disappointed at 30 days and don't return for the full series. Set the 60–90 day results timeline clearly at consultation, ideally with a visual timeline showing when to expect what.
