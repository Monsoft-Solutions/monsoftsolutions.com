The med spa industry has a growth problem — but not the kind you might expect. Most aesthetic practices are busy. Their injectors are booked out weeks. Their laser schedules fill fast in the fall. The problem is that they are leaving significant revenue on the table by undermarketing a category of services that is quietly becoming the most important profit center in functional medicine and aesthetics: wellness.

IV therapy. NAD+ infusions. Peptide programs. Hormone optimization. Vitamin injections. These services are no longer fringe. They have crossed from biohacker niche into mainstream wellness culture, driven by longevity medicine research, celebrity endorsements, and a demographic of health-conscious high earners who want to feel as good as they look.

The challenge is that most med spas offer wellness services but market them like an afterthought. A line item on the website menu. A placard in the waiting room. The practices that have figured out wellness marketing — that treat it with the same strategic discipline they apply to injectables and laser — are seeing per-visit revenue increase by 30 to 60 percent and retention rates climb because clients come back every few weeks for infusions instead of every few months for Botox.

This guide is the wellness marketing playbook for med spas ready to build a real program around these services.

## Why Wellness Services Are Different to Market

Before diving into tactics, it is worth understanding why wellness marketing requires a different approach than marketing cosmetic treatments.

**The trust barrier is higher.** When someone books a Botox appointment, they know exactly what they are getting — a well-understood treatment with decades of clinical data. IV therapy and peptides are newer to the mainstream. Prospective clients have questions: What exactly goes into that drip? Is this safe? What will it actually do for me? Your marketing has to answer those questions before they become objections.

**The benefits are felt, not seen.** Aesthetic treatments produce visible results you can photograph and share. "Before and after energy and focus" is harder to capture on Instagram. Wellness service marketing has to lean harder on client language — testimonials that describe how people feel, what they can now do, what problems are solved — rather than visual transformation.

**The purchase decision is faster.** Someone researching Botox may take months to book. Someone who just heard about IV hydration from a friend, or who is dragging through a business trip and sees your "Recovery Drip" on Google, will often book same-day. Your marketing funnel needs to accommodate both long-cycle education and short-cycle impulse capture.

**The repeat visit potential is massive.** A Botox client comes back every three to four months. An IV hydration client who finds a formula they love can come weekly. NAD+ protocol clients return monthly. Wellness services, marketed correctly, transform your revenue from transactional to recurring.

## The Wellness Services Worth Marketing in 2026

Not all wellness services are equal in market demand or margin. Understanding which services to lead with shapes everything from your content strategy to your ad spend.

**IV Hydration Therapy** remains the highest-volume wellness service for most practices. The demand is well established, the demographic reach is broad (athletes, executives, busy parents, event recovery, immune support), and the marketing angles are numerous. Average revenue per session: $150–$350. Lead with this to build traffic and introduce clients to your wellness program.

**NAD+ Infusions** are becoming the premium anchor of every serious wellness program. NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme central to cellular energy, DNA repair, and neurological function. The research on NAD+ and longevity has hit mainstream publications. Clients who invest in NAD+ infusions are highly educated, high-income, and intensely loyal to the practice that delivers results. Average revenue per session: $400–$900 for full-dose protocols. This is your premium product.

**Peptide Programs** are the fastest-growing segment of functional wellness. Peptides like BPC-157 (gut healing and recovery), Semaglutide and Tirzepatide (GLP-1 weight management), TB-500 (tissue repair), and CJC-1295/Ipamorelin (growth hormone secretagogues) are moving from elite biohacker circles into mainstream affluent wellness. Average revenue per program: $300–$1,200/month. Recurring revenue, high margin, and a client who comes back every four weeks minimum.

**Vitamin Injection Therapy** — B12 shots, lipotropic injections, Vitamin D, glutathione pushes — serve as accessible entry points. Lower price point ($25–$75 each), quick administration, easy add-on at checkout. Market these as the gateway. Get clients in for a B12 shot. They walk out knowing what else you offer.

**Hormone Optimization** (testosterone replacement, DHEA, bioidentical hormone therapy) is a longer-sales-cycle, higher-commitment service that requires specific licensing in some states. If you offer it, it commands premium pricing and exceptional retention — but it requires dedicated educational content and longer patient journeys to convert.

## Building the Right Messaging Foundation

Before you run a single ad or post a single piece of content, you need the right messaging framework. Wellness marketing fails most often not because the tactics are wrong, but because the messaging is vague.

### Define Your Wellness Identity

Are you a longevity-focused practice targeting executives and high-performers who want to optimize their bodies the way they optimize their businesses? Are you a recovery-focused practice for athletes, weekend warriors, and active professionals? Are you a beauty-wellness hybrid targeting your existing aesthetics clientele with treatments that help them look AND feel better?

Your messaging must reflect a coherent identity. "We offer IV therapy and peptides" is not an identity. "We help high-performing professionals in Naples stay sharp, recover faster, and age on their own terms" is an identity.

### Lead With Outcomes, Not Ingredients

Most wellness service marketing commits the same mistake: it describes the product instead of the outcome. "Myers Cocktail IV drip with Vitamin C, B-complex, magnesium, and zinc" means nothing to someone who does not already know what those ingredients do.

Translate every service into outcomes:

- _Myers Cocktail_ → "Feel the shift within 24 hours. Better energy, sharper focus, and the kind of recovery that used to take a week."
- _NAD+ Infusion_ → "The cellular reboot that has Silicon Valley executives flying in from across the country. Full protocol. Real results."
- _Semaglutide Program_ → "Medical weight loss that works. Clinically supervised, personalized, and built around your body."

Every piece of marketing content — ad copy, social posts, email subject lines, landing page headlines — should lead with the outcome.

### Use Client Language, Not Clinical Language

Your clients describe their results in specific, emotional terms. "I slept better than I have in years." "I finally feel like myself again." "My brain fog just... lifted." "I had energy to actually work out after work."

Collect this language through post-visit surveys, follow-up calls, and review prompts. Then use it verbatim in your marketing. Client language converts. Clinical language educates but rarely converts.

## Digital Marketing Channels That Work for Wellness

### Google Search: Capture High-Intent Local Demand

People actively searching for IV therapy, NAD+ infusions, and peptide programs in your area are already warm. They are past the awareness stage. Google Search Ads capture them at the moment of maximum intent.

**High-converting keyword groups to target:**

- IV therapy near me, IV hydration [city], IV drip bar [city]
- NAD+ therapy [city], NAD+ infusion near me
- Peptide therapy [state or city], semaglutide [city], GLP-1 [city]
- Energy infusion near me, vitamin drip [city]
- Recovery IV near me, hangover IV drip [city]

Set up separate ad groups for each service category. Write specific ad copy that matches the search intent — someone searching "NAD+ therapy Naples" should see an ad about your NAD+ program, not a generic wellness page.

**Landing pages matter.** Do not send search traffic to your homepage or your general services page. Build dedicated landing pages for each primary service: IV therapy, NAD+ infusions, peptide programs. Each page should include: a clear headline stating the benefit, what to expect during the session, client testimonials, credentials and safety information, and a single call to action (book a consultation or call now).

### Google Business Profile: Local Discovery

Your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) is especially important for wellness services because IV therapy and similar services have strong local search intent. Optimize your GBP specifically for wellness:

- Add wellness services as individual offerings with descriptions and prices
- Update your business description to mention wellness services explicitly
- Post regularly about wellness topics — new protocols, client results (with consent), educational content
- Collect reviews that specifically mention wellness services — these appear in local search results and heavily influence booking decisions

### Instagram and TikTok: Education and Aspirational Content

Instagram and TikTok are where wellness clients discover practices, not just where they engage with ones they already know. Your content strategy here should be heavily educational.

**Content formats that perform well for wellness:**

- **"What's in my drip" series**: Short-form video showing what goes into a specific IV formula and why each ingredient is included. Performs extremely well on TikTok and Reels.
- **Before/after energy timelines**: Client testimonials describing how they felt before NAD+ therapy and at 24 hours, 72 hours, one week. No photos required.
- **Myth-busting content**: "5 things people get wrong about IV therapy" consistently gets high engagement because it addresses objections before they become barriers.
- **Provider education**: Short clips of your medical director or practitioners explaining the science behind NAD+, peptides, or specific protocols. Builds authority and trust.
- **Behind-the-scenes preparation**: The clinical preparation of IV formulas, quality of ingredients, sterile technique. This converts skeptics by showing medical rigor.

HIPAA compliance applies here. No patient identifiers without explicit written consent. See our [HIPAA-compliant social media marketing](/blog/hipaa-compliant-social-media-marketing) guide for the full framework.

### Email Marketing: Educate and Convert Your Existing Database

Your existing aesthetics clients are your best wellness prospects. They trust you. They have already given you their contact information. They come back regularly. They just may not know you offer wellness services, or they may need education to understand why these services are relevant to them.

Set up an email sequence specifically for wellness:

**Wellness introduction email**: Send to your entire active client list. Subject line: "Something new (and it's not Botox)." Brief intro to your wellness program, three key services, and a special introductory offer for existing clients.

**Educational nurture sequence** (3-5 emails over 3-4 weeks):

- Email 1: What is NAD+ and why people who are already health-conscious are adding it
- Email 2: IV therapy — who it's for, what it feels like, what results to expect
- Email 3: Client story — real outcomes from a real client (with consent)
- Email 4: Peptides 101 — the GLP-1 revolution and what else is available
- Email 5: Your wellness consultation offer — book a free 15-minute consult to find the right protocol

This sequence converts significantly better than a single email blast because it meets people at different points in their awareness journey. See [automated follow-up sequences](/blog/automated-follow-up-sequences-guide) for the technical setup.

## Pricing Strategy: Position Premium, Offer Access

Wellness service pricing is an area where most practices undercut their own positioning. IV therapy and NAD+ infusions are premium medical services delivered by credentialed professionals in a clinical setting. Your pricing should reflect that — not compete with the IV bar at the mall.

### Service Menu Design

Organize your wellness menu around outcomes, not ingredients:

**Entry-level (your gateway)**: Vitamin injections and single-ingredient add-ons ($25–$75). Low commitment, instant gratification, easy yes.

**Core drips** ($150–$350): Named protocols (Myers Cocktail, Immune Boost, Athletic Recovery, Beauty Drip, Brain Boost). Clear benefit language. Most popular price point.

**Premium infusions** ($400–$900): NAD+ protocols (250mg, 500mg, full 1,000mg series), high-dose Vitamin C for immune/cancer adjunct, glutathione.

**Programs** ($300–$1,500/month): Peptide programs, hormone optimization, monthly wellness memberships. Recurring revenue. Price monthly, not per session.

### Membership Programs

If you are not offering a wellness membership tier, you are leaving recurring revenue on the table. A well-structured [membership program](/blog/med-spa-membership-programs-guide) for wellness might include:

- Monthly NAD+ session + two vitamin injections + priority booking: $299/month
- Weekly IV hydration subscription (any core drip): $499/month
- Comprehensive wellness program (drip + peptide protocol + quarterly labs): $799/month

Members come in more frequently, refer more, and churn dramatically less than pay-per-visit clients. They also anchor your monthly revenue in a way that single-session bookings cannot.

### Introductory Offers That Convert

New clients need a low-risk entry point. High-converting introductory offers for wellness:

- **Free wellness consultation**: 15 minutes with a nurse or NP to discuss goals and get a personalized protocol recommendation. Converts at 60-70% to a first booking.
- **First drip at cost**: For new clients only, offer one IV session at cost of goods ($60–$80 for a Myers, for example). The experience closes the sale for the full-price repeat.
- **Bundle and save**: Three core drips for the price of two. Commits the client to three visits before price sensitivity re-emerges.

## CRM Automation for Wellness Retention

Wellness services live and die by retention. A client who does one IV drip out of curiosity is worthless. A client on a monthly NAD+ protocol is worth $5,000–$10,000 per year. Your [CRM system](/blog/crm-automation-small-business-guide) needs to support the wellness retention journey.

**Automated reactivation for lapsed wellness clients**: Any client who has not returned in 45 days should receive an automated re-engagement message. Sequence: SMS at day 45 ("Your wellness protocol is waiting — schedule your next session"), email at day 52, final SMS at day 60 with a special offer.

**Session completion follow-up**: Within 24 hours of each wellness visit, send a check-in. "How are you feeling? Most clients notice energy improvements at the 24 and 72-hour mark. Reply and let us know." This creates conversational engagement, surfaces testimonial material, and opens the door for upselling additional sessions.

**Protocol milestone tracking**: NAD+ protocols typically run 3–6 sessions over two to four weeks. Track where each client is in their protocol and ensure no one falls off before completion. Milestone messages ("You're halfway through your NAD+ series — here's what to expect this week") reduce dropout and build commitment.

**Seasonal campaigns**: Wellness has strong seasonality. Back-to-school season (parents exhausted, immune support relevant). Tax season (executive exhaustion, cognitive performance). Post-holiday recovery (January reset energy). Summer in SWFL (heat recovery, hydration focus). Build your seasonal wellness calendar into your CRM automation so these campaigns fire automatically.

## Referral Programs Built for Wellness

Wellness clients are among the most powerful referral sources in aesthetics. When someone feels dramatically better after a NAD+ series, they talk about it. Unprompted. At dinner. In group chats. To their trainer and their business partner.

Make it easy to refer, and make it worth their while:

**Bring a Friend Program**: Existing wellness client brings a new patient for any IV service. The existing client gets a free vitamin injection; the new client gets 20% off their first drip. Both parties win.

**Referral credit tracking**: Automated credit accumulation in your CRM — for every referral that books, the referring client earns $25 toward their next visit. Track it. Email monthly balance statements. The reminder drives bookings. See our [referral program guide](/blog/referral-program-guide) for the full structure.

**Corporate Wellness Partnerships**: Every law firm, real estate office, tech company, and executive team within 20 miles of your practice has employees who would book IV therapy if they knew it existed and their employer offered it as a benefit. Pitch a corporate wellness package — monthly group IV sessions or an on-call travel kit for leadership travel. This channel is almost entirely untapped by most med spas.

## Review Strategy for Wellness Services

Reviews for wellness services are qualitatively different from aesthetic reviews. Instead of "I love my lips," you get "I felt like a completely different person within 48 hours. My energy is back, I'm sleeping through the night, and my brain fog is gone." These reviews are extraordinarily compelling to prospective wellness clients.

Capture them systematically. After each wellness session, at the 72-hour follow-up, send a direct link to your Google review page. Script the ask: "We'd love to know how you're feeling. If your experience has been positive, a quick Google review helps other people find us."

For [patient review management](/blog/patient-review-management-aesthetic-practices) at scale, automate this into your CRM follow-up sequence. The timing — at 72 hours, when clients are typically feeling the improvement — is intentional.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need separate marketing for each wellness service, or can I market wellness as a category?**
Both. A general "wellness" landing page and social presence builds category awareness. Service-specific pages and ads capture high-intent searchers looking for IV therapy or NAD+ specifically. Start with category-level content, then build service-specific assets for your highest-revenue offerings.

**How long does it take to build a consistent wellness client base?**
Most practices see meaningful wellness revenue within 60–90 days of focused marketing. A stable, recurring wellness client base — where monthly membership and return visits generate predictable revenue — typically takes 6 to 12 months to build. The infrastructure you set up in the first 90 days determines how fast you get there.

**Should we market wellness separately from our aesthetics services, or together?**
Together, strategically. Your existing aesthetics clients are your fastest path to wellness revenue — they already trust you. But wellness also attracts an adjacent demographic (the health-focused professional who is not ready for injectables but very interested in longevity medicine). Use integrated marketing to capture both: aesthetics client education sequences, plus dedicated wellness campaigns for cold audiences.

**How important is provider credentials in wellness marketing?**
Critical. The primary objection to IV therapy and peptides from skeptical prospects is "Is this safe? Who is doing this?" Lead with your medical director's credentials, your nursing team's experience, and your clinical protocols. Safety and clinical rigor are your strongest competitive differentiator against IV bars and unregulated wellness providers.

**What is the biggest mistake med spas make when marketing wellness?**
Not committing to education. Wellness clients do not come to you because of a discount — they come because they believe these services will meaningfully improve their quality of life. You earn that belief through consistent, credible educational content. Practices that post a menu and wait for bookings get nothing. Practices that teach, explain, and demonstrate expertise fill their wellness schedule.
